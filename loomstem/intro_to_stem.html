Here's the honest part most demos skip: your blocks only ever called one thing — trackPoint(height). That's a real, pre-built LoomSTEM action, the same way forward() is in the Art world. It's not LoomScript you wrote yourself; it's a tool the language gives you, and behind it, this exact formula runs every single time it's called:

    
new speed² = old speed² + 2 × gravity × (old height − new height)

    
Not a simplified version, the real one — it's why the very first drop and every drop after it can genuinely produce different numbers depending on how fast the cart already was, exactly like conservation of energy actually works. trackPoint() hides the arithmetic the same way a calculator hides how it does long division — it's still really happening, just inside a tool built to do it for you.

    
Same blocks. Same compiler. The only thing you changed was one number — real physics decided the rest, live, the same way it will for a student building their own ride, their own bowling game, or their own chemistry experiment.

    
One more thing worth noticing: every script you just built started with @engine: physics. That's a genuine declaration, not decoration — it tells the compiler which part of the language this program is allowed to use. Try the sandbox's engine dropdown with something other than "physics" and press Run: it won't silently do nothing, it'll tell you exactly why trackPoint() doesn't belong there. This is a prototype — most LoomSTEM programs don't have this line yet — but it's how a real, deliberately-declared engine boundary would work.
