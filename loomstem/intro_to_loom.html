Here's the part that's actually clever: the story remembered whether you picked up the torch, minutes of "story time" earlier, and used that to decide which choice you were even allowed to see later. That's not a script pretending to branch — it's a genuine condition, checked live, every time.

    
+ [Switch on the torch] -> sawEverything {item: torch}

  ...is read as: "only offer this choice if the player is
  carrying a torch." The mirror-image choice checks {!item: torch} --
  exactly one of the two can ever be true at once.

    
Everything else — the -> target arrows, the node headers — works exactly like it did before. Conditions are just one more real ingredient, not a different system. That's how a two-choice story and a fifty-node story with inventory, flags, and branching consequences are built the same way, just with more of it.

    
You'll also have noticed every script started with @engine: if — the same declaration line LoomSTEM's coding side uses. Worth being honest about the difference: in LoomSTEM, that line is actively checked — call a physics action under a chemistry declaration and it's caught. Here, it's currently a declared header only; Loom doesn't dispatch across multiple engines the way LoomScript calls out to physics or chemistry, so there's no mismatch to catch yet. Same line, same intent, not yet the same enforcement — that gap is real, not glossed over.
